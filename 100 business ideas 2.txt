The best 5

Ranked on: obvious job, used weekly or more, buildable on your current stack, and narrow enough that you're not fighting a funded competitor.

1. Etsy listing writer for one-of-a-kind items (#11)

What it is: the seller uploads photos and a couple of notes. The agent writes the title, description, and tags for that single item. Unlike a normal store, nothing repeats — every piece is unique, so every listing is written from zero.

Why it's strong: this is the highest-frequency job in the list. Someone listing 20–40 items a week spends hours on it, every week, forever. Daily use means very low churn. And it's genuinely underserved — every description tool assumes you're selling a repeatable branded product with existing manufacturer copy. Vintage, handmade, and art sellers have the opposite problem.

Verification is easy: did the listing get written faster and does it rank? No domain expertise required from you.

Risk: price-sensitive customers. €19–29/mo, and speed is the product.

2. Cancellation save agent for SaaS (#36)

What it is: sits on the cancel button. Asks why they're leaving, and based on the answer, offers the right thing — a pause, a downgrade, a discount, or a walkthrough of the feature they never found.

Why it's strong: the value is a number the customer can count. Save 15% of cancellations on €10k MRR and you've made them €1,500/month. That makes €99–199/mo an easy decision. Nobody has to be convinced the problem is real. And the build is simple: a conversation tree with rules for which offer fires when.

Risk: you need customers with enough cancellation volume for the numbers to show. Target businesses above roughly €5k MRR, not brand-new ones.

3. Support-to-FAQ pipeline (#80)

What it is: they connect or export their support inbox. The agent finds the questions people actually ask, writes a documented answer for each, and keeps a chat agent up to date as new questions appear.

Why it's strong: the best self-selling demo here — they upload their own emails and see their own real problems reflected back in thirty seconds. Nobody argues with their own data. It's structurally recurring because new questions arrive every month as products and policies change. And it upsells naturally: free FAQ generation, paid always-on agent.

Risk: you must build the monthly re-scan from day one, or it collapses into a one-time job that can't hold a subscription.

4. Revision request clarifier for agencies and freelancers (#64)

What it is: the client says "make it pop" or "I don't love it." The agent asks the right follow-up questions and returns an actual actionable spec — which element, what direction, what reference.

Why it's strong: unusually specific, near-zero competition, and it hits a pain every designer and developer feels physically. It's used on every single project, which means constant contact with the product. And it produces a document they send to the client, which is stickier than a dashboard.

Risk: freelancers churn faster than companies. Price at €29–49 and sell to agencies rather than solo operators where you can.

5. Proposal writer from a discovery call transcript (#62)

What it is: the agency drops in a call recording transcript. Out comes a structured proposal — scope, deliverables, timeline, assumptions, exclusions.

Why it's strong: proposals take 2–4 hours and directly precede money, so the value is unmistakable. Agencies have budget and don't quibble over €99/mo. Input is stable and simple (a transcript), output is a document. And it improves win rates, which means they attribute revenue to you.

Risk: lower frequency than the others — maybe weekly, not daily. That's fine, but it means retention depends on their deal flow, not yours.

What I'd pick for you

The Etsy listing writer for one-of-a-kind items.

Here's the reasoning, tied to what you specifically need right now.

You told me the earlier ideas were too complex, and you were right to say so. This one has no domain to learn. You don't need to understand fees, tax thresholds, or unit economics. You need to understand what makes a good Etsy listing — and you can learn that in an afternoon by reading twenty successful listings.

It also passes the test I gave you earlier, the one that actually matters. Remember: can you do one, by hand, correctly, before automating anything? Here that means taking one seller's photos and writing the listing yourself. If your version is better and faster than what they wrote, you have a product. That's a test you can run this week, and you can judge the result with your own eyes — no math to defend, no way to be confidently wrong about a number.

Technically it's the smallest build of the five: image and text in, structured text out, on the Firebase stack you already have. No integrations, no OAuth, no financial data. That last point matters more than it sounds — listing photos are far less sensitive than payout exports, so you're not carrying serious security risk while you're still learning what the product is.

And it's the only one of the five used daily. Daily use is what turns a tool into a habit, and habits are what stop people cancelling.

The honest downside: these customers don't have much money, so you'll need volume rather than high prices. That's a real constraint. But for a first product, learning from many small customers beats learning from few large ones — you'll find out what's wrong faster.

Your next step: find five Etsy sellers of vintage, handmade, or art pieces. Write one listing for each, by hand, for free. Watch what they change. What they change is your product spec.
